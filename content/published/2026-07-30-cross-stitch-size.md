# Cross Stitch Size: How to Figure Out How Big Your Finished Piece Will Be

Cross stitch size depends on three things: your fabric count, the stitch count of your pattern, and how many strands you stitch over. To find finished size, divide the stitch count by the fabric count. A 100-stitch design on 14-count fabric comes out to about 7 inches. Change the fabric count and the size changes too.

## Why Does My Cross Stitch Come Out a Different Size Than Expected?

This happens because fabric count controls size, not the pattern itself. A pattern chart is just a grid of stitches. It has no fixed size until you pick your fabric.

Think of a cross stitch pattern like a recipe that says "100 squares." It does not tell you how big each square is. The fabric count sets that. Fabric count means how many stitches fit in one inch.

If you stitch a 100-stitch by 100-stitch design on 14-count Aida, you get 100 divided by 14, which is about 7.1 inches. Stitch that same chart on 18-count fabric, and you get 100 divided by 18, which is about 5.6 inches. Same pattern, same stitches, smaller finished piece.

This trips up a lot of stitchers who buy a kit, then switch the fabric to something they already own. The design still works. It just comes out a different size than the box says.

## How Do I Calculate the Finished Size Before I Start?

Divide the stitch count by the fabric count for both width and height. Do this before you buy fabric or a frame. It takes two minutes and saves you from a piece that does not fit your hoop.

Here is the math, step by step.

1. Find the stitch count of your pattern. Most patterns list this as width x height, like "120 x 80 stitches."
2. Find your fabric count. This is the number printed on the fabric label, like 14, 16, 18, or 28.
3. Divide width by fabric count. Divide height by fabric count.
4. The answers are your finished size in inches.

Example: a pattern is 120 stitches wide and 80 stitches tall. You are stitching on 16-count Aida.

- Width: 120 ÷ 16 = 7.5 inches
- Height: 80 ÷ 16 = 5 inches

Your finished stitched area will be about 7.5 by 5 inches. Add a border of unstitched fabric around that for framing or finishing. Most stitchers add at least 2 to 3 inches on each side so there is room to mount or frame the piece.

Write this math down before you buy fabric. Cutting fabric too small is the most common beginner mistake in cross stitch, and it cannot be fixed after the fact.

## What Does Fabric Count Actually Mean?

Fabric count is the number of stitches that fit into one inch of fabric. A higher count means smaller, tighter stitches. A lower count means bigger, more open stitches.

Aida cloth is the easiest way to picture this. Aida has a grid of tiny holes woven right into the fabric, and each hole is one stitch.

- 11-count Aida: 11 stitches per inch. Big, chunky stitches. Good for beginners and kids' projects.
- 14-count Aida: 14 stitches per inch. The most common count for pattern kits.
- 16-count Aida: 16 stitches per inch. Slightly smaller stitches than 14-count.
- 18-count Aida: 18 stitches per inch. Fine detail work, harder on the eyes.
- 22-count Aida: 22 stitches per inch. Called Hardanger cloth. Very fine, used for detailed pieces.

Evenweave and linen fabrics work a little differently. They are counted in threads per inch, and you usually stitch over two threads at a time instead of one. A 28-count linen stitched over two threads gives you an effective stitch count of 14 per inch, the same finished size as 14-count Aida. This is why a pattern might say "stitched over two on 28-count linen" right in the materials list.

If your pattern does not say, assume Aida counts are stitched one-to-one, and linen or evenweave counts are stitched over two threads unless it tells you otherwise.

## Can I Make My Cross Stitch Project Bigger or Smaller Without Changing the Pattern?

Yes. Change the fabric count and the size changes automatically, with no changes to the stitching itself. Lower count fabric makes the piece bigger. Higher count fabric makes it smaller.

This is the easiest way to resize a project. You are not changing a single stitch on the chart. You are only changing the surface you stitch it on.

Say you have a pattern designed for 14-count fabric, and it comes out to 10 by 8 inches. You want it bigger, to hang as a larger piece.

Switch to 11-count fabric. Same chart, same stitch count, but now:

- Width: original stitch count ÷ 11 instead of ÷ 14
- The piece grows by roughly 25 to 30 percent in each direction

Switch to 18-count fabric instead, and the piece shrinks by about the same amount.

This trick works in both directions and costs you nothing but a different piece of fabric. It is worth checking your gauge on a scrap piece first, since brands can vary slightly even within the same stated count.

## What If I Want to Change the Stitch Count Instead of the Fabric?

You can also resize by adding or removing stitches from the pattern itself, but this takes more planning. This method changes the actual design, not just the fabric it sits on.

Some stitchers do this to fit a design into a specific hoop size, or to make a small pattern fill a bigger space without switching to loose, low-count fabric.

A few ways to do this:

**Add a border.** Keep the main design exactly as charted. Stitch a plain border of solid color stitches around the outside edge. This adds size without touching the original pattern math.

**Scale the chart digitally.** Cross stitch software can resize a chart by adding or removing stitches, similar to resizing a photo. This changes the actual design slightly and can blur fine detail, so it works best on simple, bold patterns rather than ones with lots of small color changes.

**Repeat the pattern.** For geometric or border-style designs, some patterns are made to repeat. Repeating the motif 3 times instead of 2 makes the piece longer without any resizing math.

For most stitchers, changing the fabric count is simpler and keeps the design looking exactly as the designer intended. Changing the stitch count is more of a workaround for a specific fit problem.

## Does the Number of Strands I Use Change the Size?

No, strand count does not change the finished size of the piece. It changes how thick and solid the stitches look, not how much space they cover.

This confuses a lot of new stitchers because strand count feels like it should matter the same way yarn weight matters in knitting. It does not work quite the same way in cross stitch.

Most cross stitch patterns are worked with embroidery floss, which comes as six thin strands twisted together. You separate out the number of strands the pattern calls for.

- 14-count Aida usually uses 2 strands for cross stitches
- 11-count Aida usually uses 3 strands, since the holes are bigger
- 18-count and higher usually uses 1 strand

Using more strands makes the stitch look fuller and covers the fabric better. Using fewer strands makes the stitch look thin and lets more fabric show through the gaps. But the actual size of the stitched square, the outer edge of the design, stays the same either way. Strand count is about coverage and texture, not size.

If your finished piece looks too sparse or too bulky, that is a strand count problem, not a size problem. Check your pattern's suggested strand count for your fabric count, and adjust from there based on how it looks on a test corner.

## How Do I Plan Fabric Size So I Do Not Run Short?

Add extra fabric on all four sides beyond your calculated stitch area, and always round up when buying fabric. Running short on fabric mid-project means starting over, so this step is worth doing carefully.

Here is a simple way to plan it out.

1. Calculate your stitched area in inches, using the math from earlier in this post.
2. Add 3 to 4 inches of extra fabric on every side. So a 7 by 5 inch design needs a piece of fabric at least 13 by 11 inches total.
3. If you plan to frame the piece, check your frame's opening size first, and make sure your stitched area fits inside it with room to spare.
4. If you are unsure, always buy more fabric than you think you need. Fabric is cheaper to have too much of than to run out of mid-project.

This margin gives you room to mount the piece in a hoop, stretch it for framing, or fix a mistake near the edge without running out of usable fabric.

## Quick Reference: Finished Size by Fabric Count

A 100x100-stitch design on 11-count fabric measures about 9.1 x 9.1 inches, while a 22-count comes in at around 4.5 x 4.5 inches. Use this guide as a starting point for your own patterns.


Here is a rough guide for a 100-stitch by 100-stitch square design, so you can see the size difference at a glance.

- 11-count: about 9.1 x 9.1 inches
- 14-count: about 7.1 x 7.1 inches
- 16-count: about 6.25 x 6.25 inches
- 18-count: about 5.6 x 5.6 inches
- 22-count: about 4.5 x 4.5 inches

Use this as a starting point, then run the actual math for your own pattern's stitch count. Every pattern is different, but the divide-by-fabric-count formula never changes. Measure your fabric and check your stitch count before you cut, and you will not be surprised by the final size.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I calculate the finished size of a cross stitch project?
Divide the stitch count of your pattern by the fabric count to get the size in inches. For example, a 140-stitch-wide design on 14-count Aida yields 10 inches (140 ÷ 14). Do this separately for width and height, since patterns aren't always square. Remember to factor in fabric margins for framing or finishing, typically adding 3-4 inches on each side beyond the stitched area.

### What is fabric count and how does it affect finished size?
Fabric count refers to the number of stitches per inch on your cross stitch fabric, and higher counts produce smaller finished pieces. A 14-count fabric gives 14 stitches per inch, while 18-count fits 18 stitches per inch into the same space. The same pattern stitched on 18-count will be noticeably smaller and more detailed-looking than on 14-count, since more stitches are packed into each inch.

### Does using Aida vs. evenweave change the finished size?
Yes, because Aida and evenweave are measured differently, which affects your final dimensions. Aida fabric is counted by blocks, with one stitch per block, while evenweave is stitched over two threads to create a comparable stitch size. A 14-count Aida and 28-count evenweave stitched over two threads will produce nearly identical finished sizes, so always check which counting method your pattern assumes.

### How much extra fabric should I add around my design?
Add at least 3-4 inches of blank fabric on all sides beyond your stitched area. This margin allows room for stretching onto stitching frames, professional framing, or finishing into ornaments, pillows, or other projects. If you plan to hoop-frame or use a deep frame, add even more margin. Always round up when cutting fabric, since it's easier to trim excess than to add more later.

### How can I resize a cross stitch pattern without changing the stitch count?
Switch to a different fabric count while keeping the same number of stitches to change the finished size. Moving from 14-count to 11-count fabric enlarges the design, while moving to 18 or 22-count shrinks it. The stitch pattern itself stays identical, only the physical dimensions change. Tools like fibertools.app can help you preview finished sizes across different fabric counts before you buy materials.
